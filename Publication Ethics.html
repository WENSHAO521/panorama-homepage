Policy Framework

Standards applied across the publishing programme.

Specific journals may publish additional instructions, but the publisher-level policy establishes the baseline expected across PSG journals.

01

Editorial Responsibilities

Editors of PSG journals are accountable for published content. They should ensure the quality of published material, support freedom of scholarly expression, and maintain the integrity of the academic record.

  • Editorial decisions should be based on scholarly merit, journal scope, and ethical compliance.
  • Submitted manuscripts are treated as confidential documents.
  • Editors should avoid decisions affected by personal, financial, or institutional conflicts.
02

Peer Review Integrity

Peer review is a central method by which PSG journals evaluate scholarly validity, originality, and relevance. Submitted research articles may undergo review by independent expert referees according to journal policy.

Reviewers should conduct reviews objectively and provide clear observations with supporting arguments.
03

Originality & Plagiarism

Authors must ensure that submitted work is original and that sources are appropriately acknowledged. Plagiarism in all forms constitutes unethical publishing behavior and is unacceptable.

Manuscripts should not be submitted simultaneously to multiple journals unless explicitly permitted by the relevant journal policy.

04

Conflicts of Interest

Authors, reviewers, editors, and editorial board members must disclose conflicts of interest when fulfilling their roles in the publication process.

Disclosures may include financial, institutional, collaborative, personal, or other relationships that could reasonably be perceived to influence judgment.

05

Corrections & Retractions

If a published work contains a significant error, PSG or the relevant journal may initiate investigation and, if necessary, publish an erratum, corrigendum, expression of concern, or retraction.

Correction decisions should be proportionate to the issue identified and should preserve transparency in the scholarly record.
