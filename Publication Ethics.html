Ethics.

Upholding academic integrity, rigorous peer review, and editorial transparency in accordance with recognized global publishing guidelines.

Ethical publishing is the foundation of scholarly communication. Panorama Scholarly Group expects authors, reviewers, and editors to protect the integrity of the academic record.

01

Editorial Responsibilities

Editors of PSG journals are accountable for everything published in their journals. They must ensure the quality of published material, champion freedom of expression, and maintain the integrity of the academic record.

  • Editorial decisions are based solely on the manuscript’s intellectual content, independent of the authors' race, gender, sexual orientation, religious belief, ethnic origin, citizenship, or political philosophy.
  • Editors will not disclose information about a submitted manuscript to anyone other than the corresponding author, reviewers, and the publisher, as appropriate.
02

Peer Review Integrity

Peer review is the essential method by which PSG ensures the validity of scholarly publishing. Submitted research articles undergo rigorous peer review by independent, anonymous expert referees.

Reviewer Obligations

Reviewers must treat received manuscripts as confidential documents. They should not use unpublished materials disclosed in a submitted manuscript for their own research without the author's explicit written consent. Reviews should be conducted objectively, with observations formulated clearly and supported by arguments that authors can use to improve the paper.

03

Originality & Plagiarism

Authors must ensure that they have written entirely original works. Submitting an original text entails full accountability for its intellectual integrity. Plagiarism in all its forms constitutes unethical publishing behavior and is unacceptable.

Authors are encouraged to disclose previous submission histories when doing so helps the editorial board understand the manuscript’s academic review history.
04

Conflicts of Interest

All participants in the peer-review and publication process, including authors, reviewers, editors, and editorial board members, must consider and disclose conflicts of interest when fulfilling their roles. Authors must disclose financial or other substantive conflicts of interest that might be construed to influence the results or interpretation of their manuscript.

05

Corrections & Retractions

When an author discovers a significant error or inaccuracy in their published work, it is the author’s obligation to promptly notify the journal editor or publisher and cooperate with the editor to retract or correct the paper. If the editor or publisher learns from a third party that a published work contains a significant error, PSG will initiate a thorough investigation and, if necessary, publish an erratum, corrigendum, or initiate a retraction in accordance with COPE guidelines.
