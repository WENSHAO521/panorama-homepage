PSG POLICY STATEMENT

Publication
Ethics &
Malpractice.

Supporting responsible scholarly publishing through editorial transparency, fair peer review, and clear procedures for correction, concern, and retraction.

gavel

"Panorama Scholarly Group (PSG) is committed to maintaining high standards of publication ethics. Our ethical framework is informed by recognized international guidance, including the Committee on Publication Ethics (COPE) principles."

01

Duties of
Editors

  • Editorial Decisions: Editors are responsible for determining which submissions are suitable for publication, based on scholarly merit, relevance to the journal, originality, methodological soundness, and the integrity of the work.

  • Fair & Impartial Assessment: Manuscripts should be evaluated for their intellectual content without discrimination based on race, gender, sexual orientation, religious belief, ethnic origin, citizenship, institutional affiliation, or political viewpoint.

  • Confidentiality: Editors and editorial staff must not disclose information about a submitted manuscript to anyone other than the corresponding author, reviewers, potential reviewers, editorial advisers, and the publisher, as appropriate.

  • Conflicts of Interest: Editors should recuse themselves from handling submissions in which they have competing interests. Unpublished material disclosed in a manuscript must not be used in an editor’s own research without the author’s explicit written consent.

02

Duties of
Authors

  • Reporting Standards: Authors should present an accurate account of the research performed and an objective discussion of its significance. Underlying data should be represented honestly and without fabrication, falsification, or inappropriate manipulation.

  • Data Access & Retention: Authors may be asked to provide supporting data, materials, or documentation for editorial review where appropriate and should retain relevant records for a reasonable period after publication.

  • Originality & Attribution: Authors should ensure that submitted work is original and that the words, ideas, data, or findings of others are properly cited. Plagiarism in any form is unacceptable. PSG journals may use plagiarism screening tools, including iThenticate, where applicable.

  • Redundant Submission: Authors should not submit the same manuscript, or manuscripts describing substantially the same research, to more than one journal at the same time.

  • Authorship & Contribution: Authorship should be limited to individuals who have made a significant scholarly contribution to the conception, design, execution, interpretation, or writing of the study. All those who qualify as authors should be listed, and all listed authors should approve the submitted version.

03

Duties of
Reviewers

  • Contribution to Decisions: Peer review assists editors in making editorial decisions and may also help authors improve the quality, clarity, and scholarly value of their manuscripts.

  • Promptness & Competence: Invited reviewers who feel unqualified to assess a submission, or who know that they cannot provide a timely review, should inform the editor promptly and decline the invitation.

  • Confidentiality: Manuscripts received for review must be treated as confidential documents and must not be shared, discussed, or used for personal advantage except as authorized by the editor.

  • Objectivity: Reviews should be conducted objectively and respectfully. Personal criticism of the author is inappropriate. Reviewers should support their evaluations with clear reasoning and relevant scholarly arguments.

  • Acknowledgment of Sources: Reviewers should identify relevant published work not cited by the authors and should disclose any competing interests that could affect their judgment.

§ Protocol 04

Corrections, Concerns,
and Retractions

Where concerns arise regarding the accuracy, integrity, or ethical status of a published work, PSG and the relevant journal editors will assess the matter in accordance with applicable editorial policies. Appropriate actions may include clarification, correction, expression of concern, or retraction, depending on the nature and severity of the issue.
