Panorama Scholarly Group/Ridgeline/About

The publishing identity behind Ridgeline.

How Panorama Scholarly Group's technology, engineering and AI imprint is organized, what its journals look for in a manuscript, and how it relates to the group's other four imprints.

About Ridgeline

Ridgeline is Panorama Scholarly Group's imprint for technology, engineering and artificial intelligence research. It was established to give applied, systems-oriented and computationally grounded scholarship an editorial home organized around subject matter rather than around any single journal's individual history — the same logic that separates the group's medicine imprint, Health Nexus, from its governance imprint, Charter, or its humanities imprint, Threnody.

The imprint groups three journals — Panorama Frontier Review, AI & Future Society, and the Journal of Engineering Systems & Applications — under one editorial identity, one set of publishing standards, and one point of discovery for authors and readers working across this territory. Each journal retains its own aims and scope, its own editorial board, and its own review process; Ridgeline itself does not review or accept manuscripts.

Our Mission

Ridgeline exists to give applied technology, engineering and AI research a publication venue judged on implementation and evidence rather than novelty alone. The imprint's working assumption is that a system which has been built, tested, deployed or rigorously modeled tells the scholarly record something a purely speculative proposal cannot.

This does not exclude theoretical or forward-looking work — AI & Future Society, in particular, engages directly with governance and policy questions that are inherently anticipatory — but even there, the expectation is argument grounded in evidence: existing systems, documented incidents, or formally specified models, rather than unsupported prediction.

Editorial Identity

Ridgeline's editorial identity is deliberately narrower than "technology" as a general category. It is not a venue for product announcements, marketing case studies, or unreviewed technical reports; every article published under the Ridgeline name passes through the same structured peer-review process that applies across Panorama Scholarly Group. The imprint's restrained editorial language — oriented toward engineering and systems thinking rather than consumer technology — is intended to signal that positioning to authors and readers before they read a single article.

Research Philosophy

Ridgeline's editorial boards look for three things above novelty: a clearly specified system or method, evidence of how it performs or behaves, and honesty about its limitations. A paper proposing a new architecture, protocol or governance mechanism is expected to describe what was actually built or formally analyzed, not only what a future version might do.

This philosophy extends to negative and null results. A rigorously conducted study showing that an approach does not work, or works only under narrow conditions, is a legitimate contribution to the engineering and AI literature, and Ridgeline's journals do not filter for positive outcomes alone.

Scope & Disciplines

Across its three journals, Ridgeline's scope spans artificial intelligence, engineering systems, emerging technologies, computational science, technology governance, and applied interdisciplinary research that does not sit neatly inside any single one of those categories — see Research Domains on the Ridgeline homepage for a fuller description of each area.

Panorama Frontier Review takes the broadest remit of the three, publishing multidisciplinary frontier scholarship that engages with emerging developments relevant to Ridgeline's readership. AI & Future Society is scoped specifically to AI ethics, governance, regulation and the societal effects of intelligent systems. The Journal of Engineering Systems & Applications is scoped to engineering systems and applied technology research with an implementation or applications component. Authors uncertain which journal fits a given manuscript should consult Journals or Submit, or contact the editorial team directly.

Applied Research and Emerging Technologies

A recurring editorial question for any technology imprint is how to treat work on tools, methods or systems that do not yet have an established literature of their own. Ridgeline's approach is to evaluate such submissions on methodological rigor rather than on whether the underlying technology has matured into a recognized subfield. A well-specified early study of an emerging robotics platform, an edge-computing architecture, or a novel fabrication method is assessed the same way a submission in a longer-established area would be: on the soundness of its method, the honesty of its reporting, and the clarity of its contribution.

Editorial Independence

Ridgeline is an imprint, not an editorial board. Publication decisions for every article are made by the editorial board of the journal it was submitted to — Panorama Frontier Review, AI & Future Society, or the Journal of Engineering Systems & Applications — not by imprint-level or group-level staff. This separation matters most for AI & Future Society, whose subject matter includes the governance and regulation of technology companies and public institutions: editorial independence from any commercial or governmental interest in that subject matter is a precondition for the journal's credibility, not an incidental detail.

Panorama Scholarly Group provides shared infrastructure — peer-review tooling, open-access licensing, DOI registration, indexing outreach and preservation — without exercising editorial control over individual acceptance decisions.

Relationship with Panorama Scholarly Group

Ridgeline is one of five imprints operated by Panorama Scholarly Group, alongside Health Nexus (medicine and health sciences), Verdant Science (environment, sustainability and life sciences), Charter (policy, law and governance) and Threnody (humanities, arts and philosophy). The group provides the publishing infrastructure shared across all five — submission systems, peer-review workflow, open-access licensing, Crossref DOI registration, indexing outreach, and long-term digital preservation — and maintains group-wide policy on publication ethics and research integrity that every imprint's journals operate within.

What differs by imprint is subject-matter focus and the specific disciplinary judgment applied at the editorial-board level; Ridgeline's boards understand engineering and AI research in a way a governance-focused editorial board would not, and vice versa.

Publishing Principles

Ridgeline's journals commit to structured peer review with communicated timelines, open access publication under Creative Commons licensing, persistent identification through Crossref DOI registration, indexing outreach to extend discovery beyond each journal's own readership, and long-term digital preservation through CLOCKSS and the PKP Preservation Network. Corrections and, where warranted, retractions are handled according to the group's shared publication-ethics framework, so the published record remains accountable after the fact rather than fixed and unreviewable. The full detail of how these commitments apply to Ridgeline specifically is set out on Standards.
