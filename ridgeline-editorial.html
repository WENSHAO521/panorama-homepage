Panorama Scholarly Group/Ridgeline/Community

The people who review and edit Ridgeline research.

Ridgeline's three journals are edited and reviewed by an international community of engineers, computer scientists and technology-policy scholars, organized at the journal level rather than as a single combined board.

Editorial Community

Each Ridgeline journal — Panorama Frontier Review, AI & Future Society, and the Journal of Engineering Systems & Applications — is guided by its own editorial board rather than a single board shared across the imprint. What connects them is not a shared roster but a shared standard: every board operates to the same peer-review framework, the same disclosure and conflict-of-interest expectations, and the same commitment to editorial independence, described in full on Standards.

Academic Editors

Editors make the substantive calls: whether a manuscript fits the journal's scope, who reviews it, and what the review record supports as a final decision. Ridgeline looks for editors with an active research or practice background in the journal's subject area — engineering, computer science, or technology policy, depending on the title — since editorial judgment on a technical manuscript depends on genuinely understanding the work being evaluated, not just administering a review workflow.

Peer Reviewers

Reviewers are the working core of peer review: assessing a manuscript's methods, evidence and presentation, and returning feedback specific enough for both the editor and the author to act on. Ridgeline's journals draw reviewers from active researchers and practitioners in the relevant subfield, matched to a manuscript by subject rather than assigned generically, and expect the same confidentiality and conflict-of-interest disclosure asked of editors.

Early-Career Scholars

Reviewing is one of the more direct ways an early-career researcher builds editorial experience and visibility within a field, and Ridgeline's journals do not require a senior title to review or, in time, to join a board. What matters is demonstrated expertise in the manuscript's subject area — a strong publication or practice record in the relevant technical domain counts, regardless of career stage.

International Collaboration

Because applied AI, engineering and emerging-technology research is not concentrated in a single country or research tradition, Ridgeline's editorial boards and reviewer pools are not either. Authors, editors and reviewers working anywhere in the world are welcome to participate, provided they can engage with a manuscript's technical content and meet the same disclosure and confidentiality standards asked of every participant.

Join an Editorial Board

Editorial board applications are reviewed by the relevant journal against its current editorial needs — subject-area coverage, capacity, and fit with the journal's scope. Apply through the group's editorial board application, indicating which Ridgeline journal and subject area you're applying for.

Become a Reviewer

Reviewer interest is registered through the same application form, and does not require prior editorial-board experience. Journals draw on the reviewer pool as manuscripts in a given subject area arrive, so registering does not guarantee an immediate assignment.

Research Partnerships

Institutions, research groups and conferences occasionally propose partnerships with a Ridgeline journal — a special collection, a co-organized call for papers, or a similar collaboration. These are handled by the relevant journal's editorial office directly; start with a short outline of the proposal to the journal's editorial-office address below.
