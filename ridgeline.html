02 / About Ridgeline

Engineering ideas into scholarly impact.

Ridgeline is the technology, engineering and artificial intelligence imprint of Panorama Scholarly Group, established to give applied and systems-oriented research a publishing home distinct from the group's medicine, environmental science, governance and humanities imprints. Where much of the technology literature splits between purely theoretical venues and purely commercial product writing, Ridgeline occupies the space between: work that has been built, deployed, measured or formally modeled, and that a practicing engineer or computer scientist would recognize as addressing a real system rather than an abstraction of one.

The imprint currently comprises three journals, a multidisciplinary frontier-scholarship title, a venue for AI ethics and governance research, and a journal dedicated to engineering systems and applications, each maintaining its own aims and scope, its own editorial board, and its own decision-making process. Ridgeline does not operate a single combined editorial board across these titles; grouping them under one imprint is an organizational and discovery convenience for authors and readers, not a merger of their editorial judgment. A manuscript submitted to one Ridgeline journal is evaluated on that journal's own terms.

What the three journals share is Panorama Scholarly Group's publishing infrastructure: a common peer-review framework, open-access licensing under Creative Commons, Crossref DOI registration, indexing outreach, and long-term digital preservation commitments. This shared foundation lets a focused imprint operate to the same publishing standards as a larger, more established press, without asking each journal's editorial board to build that infrastructure independently.

Ridgeline's authorship is international by default. Because the subject matter, applied AI, engineering systems, emerging technology, is not tied to a single research tradition or regulatory jurisdiction, the journals welcome submissions describing work conducted, tested or deployed anywhere in the world, provided it meets the same standard of methodological transparency. Throughout, the emphasis is on research with a working implementation, a measured outcome, or a rigorously specified proposal, rather than speculative commentary on where a field might be headed.

04 / Research Domains

The intellectual territory Ridgeline covers.

Every Ridgeline journal sits somewhere inside these six areas; none is exclusive to a single title.

01

Artificial Intelligence

Machine learning, natural language processing, computer vision, and the systems engineering required to deploy AI reliably. Work on model behavior and evaluation sits alongside research on the governance implications of deployed AI.

02

Engineering Systems

Mechanical, electrical, industrial and systems-engineering research addressing the design, integration and performance of engineered systems, from component-level analysis to full system architectures.

03

Emerging Technologies

Technologies at an early or transitional stage of adoption, including robotics, embedded and edge computing, and novel materials and fabrication methods, not yet served by an established specialist literature.

04

Computational Science

Computational methods, modeling and simulation applied to technical and scientific problems, including the algorithms and infrastructure that make large-scale computation tractable.

05

Technology Governance

The regulatory, ethical and institutional questions raised by AI and emerging technology: accountability, safety, standards-setting, and how technical systems interact with the people and institutions they affect.

06

Applied Interdisciplinary Research

Scholarship sitting across the boundary of engineering, computer science and adjacent fields, where the contribution does not fit neatly inside a single discipline's established venues.

05 / Why Ridgeline

What every Ridgeline journal commits to.

Editorial
01

Independent editorial evaluation

Each Ridgeline journal maintains its own editorial board of practicing engineers and computer scientists, who make final decisions on scope fit, review outcomes and acceptance. Editorial judgment sits with the journal, not the imprint.

02

Transparent peer review

Every submission that passes initial screening goes through structured peer review, with editorial timelines communicated to authors and reviewer feedback returned in full.

03

Open access, no reader paywalls

Articles publish under Creative Commons (CC BY) licensing, free to read, download and reuse from the day of publication, with no subscription or access fee charged to readers.

04

Rolling submission

Ridgeline journals do not work to fixed issue deadlines. Manuscripts are received, reviewed and published continuously, so acceptance does not wait on the next scheduled issue.

Infrastructure
05

Persistent identification

Every accepted article receives a Crossref DOI, registered under prefix 10.63802, giving it a permanent, resolvable citation record independent of where it is hosted.

06

Indexed for discovery

Published research is indexed in Google Scholar, extending each article's reach beyond the journal's own readership and into the discovery tools researchers already use.

07

Archived for the long term

Content is preserved in CLOCKSS and the PKP Preservation Network, two independent archives designed to keep published research accessible even if a title or its publisher were to discontinue.

08

A correctable scholarly record

Published articles remain open to correction. Where an error is identified after publication, Ridgeline follows the group's corrections and retractions policy to update the record transparently.

07 / Publication Journey

From journal selection to a discoverable, cited record.

The complete step-by-step guidance for each stage is in the Author Guide →

01

Journal Selection

Identify the Ridgeline journal whose aims and scope most closely match the manuscript's subject and contribution.

02

Manuscript Submission

Submit the manuscript, with required metadata and declarations, through the chosen journal's own submission portal.

03

Editorial Screening

An editor checks scope fit, completeness and adherence to basic ethical and formatting requirements before review begins.

04

Peer Review

Reviewers assess the manuscript's methods, contribution and presentation, returning written feedback to guide the decision.

05

Revision

Authors respond to reviewer and editor comments, submitting a revised manuscript with a point-by-point response where requested.

06

Editorial Decision

The journal's editorial board reaches a decision, acceptance, further revision, or rejection, based on the review and response.

07

Production

Accepted manuscripts move through copyediting, typesetting and metadata preparation ahead of formal publication.

08

Publication & Discovery

The article publishes open access with a registered DOI, and is submitted for indexing and long-term preservation.

Research belongs where it can be discovered.
